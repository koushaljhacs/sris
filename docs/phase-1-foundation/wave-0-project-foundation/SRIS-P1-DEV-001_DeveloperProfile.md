This document records the profile of the Lead Architect of SRIS (Sarathi Railway Information System). It covers academic background, certifications, technical skills, working method, and learning goals. It is a formal register document, not a personal CV.

The Lead Architect is the author and lead of SRIS (Sarathi Railway Information System), an independent academic project at GLA University. SRIS is a unified railway management platform and is not affiliated with Indian Railways, CRIS, or IRCTC. The project's vision, mission, scope, deliverables, success criteria, and constraints are defined in [SRIS-P1-GOV-005](./SRIS-P1-GOV-005_ProjectVisionAndScopeDocument.md) — Project Vision and Scope Document, and are not repeated here.

## 6. Role in Phase 1

The Lead Architect leads Phase 1 across management, architecture, documentation, and technology decisions, and develops alongside the team across frontend, backend, database, and infrastructure. He is the sole contributor to the design half of the phase; the wider team joins active development from Week 3 and builds against the frozen contracts and the frontend design he produces. The supervisor evaluates at the end of the phase and does not take part in day-to-day decisions. Individual roles are recorded in [SRIS-P1-DEV-002](./SRIS-P1-DEV-002_RolesAndResponsibilitiesDocument.md) — Roles and Responsibilities Document, and the schedule is recorded in [SRIS-P1-GOV-007](./SRIS-P1-GOV-007_ProjectManagementPlan.md) — Project Management Plan.

---

## 7. AI-Assisted Working Method

The Lead Architect uses AI assistants across documentation, research, and design work, selecting the assistant that best suits the task at hand rather than assigning a fixed tool to each activity. Human judgement is retained for architecture, contract design, final wording, and every decision that affects project direction. AI output is treated as a draft that is reviewed, corrected, and approved before it enters the register. The AI-assisted sessions on the project are recorded in [SRIS-P1-NOTES-AI-001](../wave-9-supporting-artifacts/SRIS-P1-NOTES-AI-001_AIConsultantLog.md) — AI Consultant Log.

## 8. Working Environment

The Lead Architect works primarily on Windows and administers a local Linux server over SSH. Day-to-day tooling is Git and GitHub for version control, VS Code and Notepad for editing, and PM2 for process management. The local server is constrained to 5.1 GB of RAM, which is the operative hardware limit for the project. The project's chosen languages, frameworks, data stores, and infrastructure products are recorded in [SRIS-P1-ARCH-008](../wave-2-architecture/SRIS-P1-ARCH-008_TechnologyStackADR.md) — Technology Stack ADR.

## 9. Learning Goals

**Phase 1 goals.** Complete the Phase 1 Foundation within the eight-week deadline alongside the project team. Use the phase as a learning period: practise new skills, identify gaps in the team's current capabilities, and improve against them as the phase progresses. The aim is not only to deliver the five Phase 1 deliverables but to leave every team member stronger at the end of the phase than at the start.

**Post-Phase 1 goals.** Apply four years of study and AI-assisted tooling to one substantial engineering work, and demonstrate the architectural judgement and design discipline that remain the engineer's responsibility regardless of the tools used. Build a portfolio piece that shows a real architecture and a traceable document set rather than a simple CRUD application. Understand, by building it, how the transactional engine of a railway reservation system is designed.
